When people talk about "personalised" health information, it is tempting to imagine a tool that shows you only what applies to you and quietly drops the rest. It sounds helpful. For medicines, it can be risky.

The Gravitate-Health project took a different approach. Its Gravitate Lens was designed to **focus, but not conceal or filter,** the approved information about a medicine. That distinction is worth spelling out.

## Why not simply filter?

The package leaflet that comes with a medicine has been reviewed and approved by regulators. Each section is there for a reason. If software decides that a section "doesn't apply" and removes it, a few things can go wrong:

- The software might be wrong, for example because a person's records are incomplete or out of date.
- The person loses the ability to check for themselves, or to read about a situation that might apply later.
- The information is no longer the approved information. It has become something new that nobody reviewed as a whole.

## What focusing looks like

Focusing keeps the full, approved content intact and changes what comes first. Using information a person has chosen to share, such as a summary of their health record, a tool can highlight the parts most likely to matter to them, add plain-language explanations, and move them up the page.

Someone who is pregnant might see the section on pregnancy first. Someone taking several medicines might see interactions highlighted. Everything else is still there, one step away.

## Why it matters for trust

Trust in health information comes partly from knowing nothing is being kept from you. A focusing approach respects the person's right to the complete picture and the regulator's role in approving it, while still recognising that a thirty-paragraph leaflet is not easy to read when you are unwell.

It also sets a useful standard for anyone building digital health tools: make information easier to use, and never make it less complete.

## The questions that remain

There is still a lot to learn. Which ways of focusing help people most? How do you explain why something has been highlighted? How do you make this work for people with low digital confidence, or in many languages? These are exactly the kinds of questions Gravitate wants to explore with patients, professionals, researchers and the organisations that publish medicines information.
